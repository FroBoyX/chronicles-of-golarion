## Evidence Boundary and GM Reconstruction

### Recovered / strongly evidenced

- Erastus 15 is a normal-weather day between the storm aftermath and the Erastus 16 crab-reef task.
- Rak's Patch scene belongs late on Erastus 15 around the night-watch transition.
- Patch identifies herself as Patch / Patches, boatswain's mate.
- Patch speaks approvingly of Scourge's use of the whip.
- Patch asks Rak whether he drank his rum; Rak claims he did and says he always does.
- Rak asks whether Patch wants company.
- Patch warns that she becomes `stabby` in her sleep, cracks her knuckles, winks, and tells Rak to meet her on the night shift.
- No recovered evidence confirms that Rak later met Patch or that a sexual encounter occurred.
- On the morning of Erastus 16, Sho is slumped and tied to the mast.
- Scourge places the whip under Sho's chin and asks whether she enjoyed the prior punishment.
- Sho recognizes from Scourge's grin that he enjoyed it.
- Scourge unlocks Sho only after the land sighting and Plugg's summons.
- Sho stands wearily and rubs her wrists.
- Later that day Sho explicitly says it was hard to swim after a **night shackled to the mast**.
- Plugg answers that she will have to mind her `pretty mouth`.
- Sho's player evidence establishes that she never willingly drank the Wormwood rum ration.
- Harvus later says he figured out Sho was a woman during one of her whippings.

### GM-locked reconstruction for the table-play gap

The surviving chat jumps from the Patch-era Erastus 15 material into the Erastus 16 morning state, leaving the punishment itself absent. The GM has chosen the following event as canon to close that table-play gap:

- Sho is caught at **dinner** refusing the evening rum ration.
- Scourge orders her to drink; she refuses and openly challenges the order.
- Scourge postpones the formal punishment until Bloody Hour rather than administering it at dinner.
- At Bloody Hour, Sho is tied to the mast and whipped for both rum refusal and verbal defiance.
- The punishment grows severe enough that Sho briefly loses control of the physical and vocal presentation she normally uses to mask her gender.
- The tell is subtle. Most crew can plausibly miss it, but a perceptive observer can notice that something does not fit the male presentation.
- Harvus notices. This is the whipping later referenced when he tells Sho that he had figured out she was a woman.
- Scourge does not need to understand what slipped. His immediate focus is that he has finally broken through some of Sho's composure.
- Sho refuses to beg or submit verbally.
- At the end of Bloody Hour, Scourge orders Sho left bound to the mast overnight.

Treat these as canon events in narrative output. Do **not** write them with uncertainty language in player-facing prose. Preserve the reconstruction label only in archive-facing metadata.

## Sho Dinner / Bloody Hour Locks

- The offense is discovered **at dinner**, not at Bloody Hour.
- Sho does not willingly drink the rum.
- Scourge catches the refusal before she can dispose of or redirect the ration.
- The confrontation is public enough that nearby conversation dies away and crew notice.
- Scourge makes it clear that Bloody Hour will settle the matter.
- Bloody Hour occurs before the night-watch changeover.
- Sho's punishment is formal and public at the mast.
- The exact number of lashes is not separately locked and need not be counted in prose.
- The narrative may show Sho enduring the first strokes with control before pain strips that control away.
- The gender-mask slip should be visible through a combination of voice, posture, clothing tension, and loss of deliberately controlled presentation, not through explicit exposure or a public declaration.
- Harvus notices the tell and keeps it to himself.
- Do not make the entire crew suddenly know Sho is a woman.

- Sho is left shackled to the mast after Bloody Hour and remains there through the night.

## Rak / Patch Scene Locks

The recovered Patch interaction belongs **during the night-watch changeover after Bloody Hour**.

- Rak approaches Patch as the night hands are taking over.
- Patch initially growls at Rak and asks what he wants.
- Patch recognizes him as the cook's mate and insults the food as possible poison.
- Patch questions whether Rak should still be working.
- Rak says the cooking is finished and claims the crew will like that night's food.
- Patch threatens to tell Scourge, then introduces herself as Patch / Patches, the boatswain's mate.
- Patch speaks approvingly of Scourge teaching through the whip.
- Patch asks whether Rak drank his rum that day.
- Rak claims he did and says he always does.
- Rak asks whether she wants company.
- Patch warns that she becomes `stabby` in her sleep.
- Rak answers by referring to the hardness of his scales.
- Patch cracks her knuckles, winks, tells Rak to meet her on the night shift, and dismisses him.
- The interaction is threatening flirtation, not proof of trust or safety.
- No evidence confirms a later encounter that night.

Sho remains physically present at the mast in the wider night scene. The narrative does not need Patch or Rak to make a point of her, but it should not forget that Bloody Hour has just happened.
