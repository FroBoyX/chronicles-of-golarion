## Narrative Log

The second clear morning felt less like relief than routine.

The bell sounded. Assignments followed. The Wormwood moved under steady wind while its crew returned to the work that had defined them before the storm.

Nothing remained to be celebrated.

Harvus worked aloft with his injured wing held close whenever the rigging allowed it. Sho hauled, scrubbed, and carried through the bruising left by the line around her waist. Mirei moved among the lower crew where the ship needed another pair of hands.

The work no longer demanded heroics.

It demanded repetition.

Rak spent the day in the galley with Kroop.

The fire behaved. Pots stayed where they were put. Knives remained on the table instead of trying to cross the room whenever the hull rolled. Ordinary food replaced emergency broth, and the crew again found enough strength to complain about what they were served.

Rak's palms still carried the rescue lines.

The burns had begun closing, but every heavy handle and tight grip opened the memory again. Kroop noticed without asking and left more of the chopping to himself than usual. He offered no explanation. Rak did not require one.

By late afternoon, the cooking was far enough along that the galley no longer needed both of them every moment.

Rak left the heat behind and moved forward through the ship.

Patch Patchsalt was near the forward berths, where she could sleep before taking charge of the night shift. She did not welcome the interruption.

Her first response was a growl.

She wanted to know what Rak was doing there. She recognized him as the cook's mate and suggested the food he had served might have been poison. If he was not trying to kill the crew with supper, she wondered why he was not still working.

Rak told her the cooking was finished.

He assured her that the meal would be better that night.

Patch did not look reassured.

She asked whether he wanted her to repeat that claim to Scourge. The threat came easily, almost playfully, because she did not need to explain what Scourge could do with an excuse.

Then she introduced herself properly.

Patch. Patches. The boatswain's mate.

The title mattered more than the name.

She belonged close enough to Scourge's authority to borrow some of it. She spoke of his whip as though it were a useful teacher and expected Rak to understand the lesson.

Rak did not give her the reaction she wanted.

Patch changed questions.

She asked whether he had drunk his rum that day.

Rak claimed that he had.

He claimed he always did.

Patch watched him for a moment, weighing the answer against whatever she already knew about the cook's mate who wandered away from the galley and spoke too easily around dangerous people.

Then she said she needed to sleep.

The night watch would be hers soon.

Rak asked whether she wanted company.

The question shifted the conversation without making it safer.

Patch allowed that he could sleep beside her, but warned him that she became a little dangerous in her sleep. The threat involved knives. With Patch, most threats did.

Rak answered by drawing attention to his scales and how hard they were.

Patch cracked her knuckles.

She looked amused now.

She told him she would test that claim. He could meet her on the night shift.

Then she ordered him away so she could sleep.

Rak left with the invitation.

It sounded enough like a threat that another sailor might have refused it.

It sounded enough like a challenge that Rak did not.

The evening bell came. Rum was issued. Day workers drifted below while Patch and the night hands prepared to take their places.

Nothing in the surviving account says whether Rak returned to her after dark.

The night shift began without recording what happened between them.
