# Man's Promise Survivor Bloc and Original Voyage Discovery

## Document Status

- Type: Discovery / design analysis
- Maturity: Working proposal, not canon
- Scope: Original Man's Promise survivor faction, pre-capture commercial purpose, ownership claim, and post-mutiny destination options
- Canon status: Do not treat the recommended voyage model as locked until GM approval

## Core Correction

The original Man's Promise survivors are not controlled assets, neutral swing votes, or a subset of a generic survival faction.

They are a primary political faction with:

- a prior relationship to the ship,
- a collective claim to its identity and future,
- surviving shipboard competence,
- shared losses from the Wormwood boarding,
- reasons to oppose Plugg,
- and reasons to distrust the PCs even when cooperating against Plugg.

The practical survival problem is a constraint on every faction, not a replacement for survivor politics.

## What the Existing Evidence Establishes

- The Man's Promise was a merchant vessel before capture.
- The ship had a Rahadoumi officer and a substantial Rahadoumi survivor identity.
- Goyle was its quartermaster.
- Kahina is a rigger / warrior figure and currently the strongest speaking survivor voice.
- Ebony and Merchiel are riggers whose labor matters after the conflict.
- Dahrehn was previously a wealthy trader.
- Nor'gal is a sailor / soldier driven by revenge for his brother.
- The captain and only surviving Rahadoumi officer are dead.
- The Wormwood thoroughly looted the vessel, leaving the post-capture hold largely empty.

The archive does not yet establish:

- legal ownership,
- charter structure,
- original port of departure,
- intended destination,
- cargo,
- or whether Dahrehn owned the ship, financed the cargo, chartered space, or merely traveled aboard.

## Voyage Models

### Model A — Southbound Rahadoumi Trade Charter to Bloodcove

The Man's Promise was sailing from a Rahadoumi port toward Bloodcove, possibly with a later leg to Eleder.

Plausible outbound cargo:

- fine cloth,
- salt,
- tools and base-metal goods,
- herbal remedies,
- gemstones,
- and smaller quantities of valuable produce.

The planned return cargo could have included:

- rare hardwoods,
- precious metals,
- Mwangi luxury goods,
- artifacts,
- or other Bloodcove exports.

Strengths:

- Gives Dahrehn a strong role as principal merchant, investor, or supercargo.
- Gives the survivors a nearby intended destination and commercial contacts.
- Explains why a valuable armed merchant vessel risked pirate waters.
- Makes Bloodcove a credible post-mutiny alternative to immediate piracy.
- Allows Rickety's Squibs to remain an emergency repair stop rather than the automatic permanent conversion of the ship.

Weaknesses:

- The exact cargo must be authored.
- The reason the ship lacked an effective escort needs to be accepted as normal danger, bad luck, or overconfidence.

### Model B — Northbound Return From Bloodcove to Rahadoum

The Man's Promise had completed a southern trade voyage and was returning north with high-value Mwangi goods.

Strengths:

- Makes the vessel an especially attractive piracy target.
- Explains Dahrehn's lost wealth directly through the captured cargo.
- Gives the survivors a clear desire to return home.

Weaknesses:

- Pulls their preferred destination away from the Book Two operating area.
- Makes immediate cooperation with a pirate future more difficult.
- Requires deciding what high-value return cargo the Wormwood stole.

### Model C — Chartered Coastal Circuit

The ship served an armed merchant circuit between Rahadoum, Bloodcove, Sargava, and other western Garundi ports rather than a single out-and-back voyage.

Strengths:

- Flexible and commercially plausible.
- Gives the crew contacts in multiple ports.
- Allows the survivors to argue over whether to complete the next leg, return home, or sell the ship.

Weaknesses:

- Less emotionally clear than a specific destination.
- Risks leaving the ship's immediate purpose too diffuse.

## Recommended Working Model

Use **Model A: a southbound Rahadoumi trade charter toward Bloodcove**, with the possibility of a later Eleder leg.

Recommended structure:

- The ship belonged to a Rahadoumi merchant house or small investment consortium rather than to the crew collectively.
- Dahrehn was the principal cargo investor / supercargo or a major chartering merchant, not the captain.
- The dead captain commanded the ship under the charter.
- The executed Rahadoumi officer was the last surviving formal officer after the boarding.
- Goyle managed stores, cargo access, accounts, and issue of equipment as quartermaster.
- Kahina was a veteran rigger and armed deck defender rather than a formal officer.
- Ebony and Merchiel were experienced riggers and dependable working sailors.
- Nor'gal and Kor'al served as sailors with military experience or shipboard-defense duties.

This preserves the established job roles while explaining why Kahina and Ebony become the survivor bloc's visible post-capture faces: the formal command structure is dead, Goyle is absent until the rescue return, and Dahrehn's commercial status does not automatically make him a fighting leader.

## What the Survivors Mean by "Our Ship"

Their claim need not mean that all six legally own equal shares.

It can mean:

- this was their vessel and livelihood,
- they served under its prior captain,
- their shipmates died defending it,
- the ship's cargo and identity were taken from them,
- and neither Harrigan nor Plugg acquired moral legitimacy merely by winning a boarding action.

The likely legal / commercial claim is mixed:

- Dahrehn or his principals may own cargo or charter rights.
- A Rahadoumi trading house or consortium may hold title to the vessel.
- Goyle can speak to shipboard stores and operational continuity.
- The surviving sailors possess the practical claim of the dispossessed crew.

## Survivor Bloc Mutiny Position

The survivor bloc is likely planning its **own overthrow of Plugg**, not merely deciding whether to join a PC mutiny.

Probable structure:

- Kahina recruits or tests Sho as a weapon against Plugg and Scourge.
- Ebony coordinates the people and physical work needed to act.
- Merchiel prevents Nor'gal from forcing the action too early.
- Dahrehn waits to see whether the ship can be recovered rather than merely changing pirate captains.
- Goyle's return supplies missing legitimacy, testimony, and quartermaster authority.
- Nor'gal may support killing Plugg while still turning on Harvus or Rak afterward.

The survivor objective is not initially "make Sho captain."

It is:

1. Remove Plugg and Scourge.
2. Prevent another massacre.
3. Reassert a survivor claim to the Man's Promise.
4. Keep enough sailors alive to leave Bonewrack.
5. Decide the ship's destination and ownership only after immediate survival is secured.

## Post-Mutiny Destination Positions

### Immediate Emergency Consensus

Likely common ground:

- recover all viable crew,
- load water and food,
- repair enough damage to leave Bonewrack,
- avoid unnecessary executions,
- and establish temporary command rather than permanent ownership.

### Rickety's Squibs

Possible survivor position:

- Accept emergency repair, supplies, and recruitment if necessary.
- Reject automatic squibbing, renaming, or sale because those acts erase the Man's Promise identity and weaken the survivors' claim.
- Treat any disguise as an emergency measure requiring survivor consent.

### Bloodcove

Under the recommended voyage model, Bloodcove is the strongest survivor-preferred next destination:

- it was the original commercial destination,
- Dahrehn may have contacts there,
- it offers trade, repair, hiring, sale, and passage options,
- and it provides a place to decide whether to continue trading, sell the ship, seek return passage, or enter piracy under a negotiated charter.

### Rahadoum

Longer-term option:

- return the ship or survivors home,
- report the capture,
- seek compensation or legal recognition,
- or reconnect with the vessel's owners.

This would be difficult for openly religious or pirate PCs and need not be the immediate destination.

### Piracy

The survivor bloc should not accept piracy by default.

It could become acceptable only through negotiated terms such as:

- survivor representation in command,
- shares or compensation,
- recognition of the survivors' ship claim,
- Goyle restored as quartermaster,
- a clear ship's code,
- limits on violence and treatment of prisoners,
- and a destination vote after Bonewrack.

Nor'gal may reject this regardless of terms.

## Recommended Temporary Settlement After the Final Conflict

Do not settle permanent ownership on the beach.

Use an emergency sailing compact:

- Temporary captain or sailing commander chosen for the escape from Bonewrack.
- Goyle restored as quartermaster.
- Kahina and/or Ebony represent the original Man's Promise crew.
- Sandara or another trusted figure represents the Wormwood transfers.
- Dahrehn controls or audits surviving commercial records and property claims.
- Permanent ownership, renaming, piracy, and destination beyond the first safe port remain open for council.

This keeps the ship politically contested after Plugg falls and creates meaningful Book Two choices instead of granting the vessel automatically to the PCs.

## Decisions Still Needed

- Lock southbound or northbound voyage direction.
- Decide whether Dahrehn was supercargo, cargo investor, charterer, or vessel shareholder.
- Decide the dead captain's name and relationship to the crew.
- Decide whether Kahina and Ebony had informal seniority before capture.
- Decide the original destination: Bloodcove alone or Bloodcove followed by Eleder.
- Decide whether Rickety's can repair the ship without squibbing it and what price or political pressure follows.
- Decide what documents, cargo records, seals, or ownership papers survived the Wormwood looting and Plugg's occupation of the captain's cabin.
