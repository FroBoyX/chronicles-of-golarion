# Sho Astor and Barnabas Harrigan

## Document Status

- Type: Relationship / perception record
- Maturity: Draft, seeded from Man's Promise reward evidence, Sho player answers, and contemporaneous private character-page evidence
- Scope: Sho's warning to Harrigan, captain's reward, confused respect, guilt, and distinction between Harrigan and Plugg / Scourge
- Source basis: Sho player answers relayed by GM; `evidence/obsidian-portal/sho-astor-private-character-notes.md`; recovered Man's Promise battle and reward reconstruction
- Spoiler scope: Character-private / GM-facing until revealed in play

## Summary

Sho Astor does not view Barnabas Harrigan as safe or good, but she also does not place him in the same immediate moral category as Mr. Plugg and Master Scourge.

A recovered contemporaneous private character note states that Sho **"Has an odd sense of respect for Captain Harrigan."** This confirms that the confused respect was present during original play and is not merely a later retrospective interpretation.

Sho warned Harrigan during the Man's Promise battle on instinct. She saw an attack about to happen and acted before thinking through what it meant.

Harrigan's later reward left Sho confused, uncomfortable, and guilty rather than simply proud.

## The Warning

Sho does not fully know why she warned Harrigan.

The best current reconstruction is instinct: she saw the attack forming and warned him.

Sho often acts before considering consequences, and this was one of those moments. She did not fully account for who Harrigan was; she simply did not want to see another person taken out.

The warning worked out both fortunately and unfortunately for Sho.

Do not overread the warning as loyalty to Harrigan.

## The Reward

Harrigan's reward created confusion and discomfort.

Sho did not fully grasp how much her own life was on the line during the Man's Promise fight. Afterward, she was discomforted that her life had been spared or improved at the expense of innocent people's lives.

She reads the reward as luck mixed with guilt.

The later player clarification identifies Harrigan's reward and his protection of Sho from Plugg as major sources of Sho's moral-grey view of him.

Sho also believed that some crew, including Peppery and possibly Kroop / the cook, seemed to genuinely respect Harrigan rather than only fear him. That apparent respect made Harrigan's authority harder for Sho to dismiss as simple terror.

## The Pendant / Captain's Amulet

Sho does not treat the pendant as a clean trophy.

She most likely keeps Harrigan's amulet stashed away with her reversible cloak.

The item remains morally complicated: tied to survival, recognition, Harrigan's protection, and the deaths of innocent people.

## Distinction From Plugg and Scourge

Sho sees a distinction between Harrigan and Plugg / Scourge.

Harrigan may need to be taken down for his actions at some point.

Plugg and Scourge, by contrast, need to be taken out as soon as possible in Sho's mind.

The distinction is not absolution. It is priority, category, and moral readability. Plugg and Scourge delight in malice. Harrigan is cruel and dangerous, but his competence, reward logic, and the respect he apparently commands make him harder for Sho to categorize.

If Harrigan continues plundering innocent ships and Sho has the opportunity and ability to stop him, Sho will do so.
